# Austan Goolsbee Is Worried the Economy Is Overheating
**Podcast:** Odd lots

---

## AI Executive Summary
1. Core Thesis
The episode analyzes the current state of monetary policy in an era defined by persistent service inflation, AI-driven structural changes, and a potential paradigm shift in Federal Reserve communication strategy. Guest Austin Goolsbee argues that while long-term productivity growth from AI may raise the neutral interest rate (R-Star), short-term policy must prioritize monitoring whether inflation shocks are transitory or structurally embedded, particularly within the services sector, amidst a move away from rigid forward guidance toward market-informed reaction functions under Chair Kevin Warsh.

2. Key Arguments & Data
*   **Restrictiveness & R-Star:** Monetary policy restrictiveness is best measured by real rates (nominal rate minus inflation). Goolsbee notes that while AI is expected to increase the long-run neutral interest rate (R-Star), its short-term impact depends on whether technology-driven productivity is perceived as an "unexpected" bounty or a risk of economic overheating.
*   **Inflation Drivers:** Post-pandemic inflation was composed roughly two-thirds by supply shocks (tariffs, one-time oil price spikes) and one-third by demand. Current sticky inflation in services is not driven by tariffs or oil but represents a deeper structural concern that suggests the economy may require more restrictive policy than standard cycles suggest.
*   **Fed History:** The Fed deserves credit for its aggressive hiking cycle (2022–2023) because it avoided losing the 2% inflation anchor; evidence from tip-of-the-tip CPI (2.3%) correlated closely with the target despite high headline inflation, suggesting an "immaculate disinflation" was achievable without a deep recession.
*   **AI & Investment Impacts:** High investment in AI data centers is causing localized supply constraints (e.g., labor, construction materials in Iowa) and driving up prices, but this sectoral pressure has not yet translated to aggregate GDP or unemployment indicators showing systemic overheating.
*   **Communication Shifts:** Under Chair Warsh, there is a move toward less forward guidance (to avoid "tying hands") and more reliance on the bond market as a signal for policy reaction. The FOMC will focus more on defining the "Reaction Function" than making specific predictions. Dot plots may be repurposed to visualize the committee's worldview rather than act as binding forecasts.
*   **Dissent & Voting:** Official dissents are rare (historically low), but silent dissent exists among non-voting members who would dissent if permitted. Goolsbee expressed dissatisfaction with front-loading rate cuts due to persistent inflation uncertainty, noting he was not subjected to "mean and nasty" calls despite his opposition.

3. Actionable Takeaways
*   **Monitor Services Data:** Investors and policymakers should pay closer attention to services sector inflation trends rather than focusing solely on total CPI/PCE averages, as this appears to be the driver of persistent price pressures.
*   **Expect Communication Changes:** Anticipate a shift in Fed press conferences and FOMC minutes that prioritize explaining the reaction function over predicting future rates; do not treat current forward guidance as immutable commitments given the emphasis on Warsh’s new directive.
*   **Assess AI Risks:** View AI investment not just as a productivity booster but as a potential source of short-term supply-side inflationary pressure through resource competition, particularly in construction and utility sectors.
