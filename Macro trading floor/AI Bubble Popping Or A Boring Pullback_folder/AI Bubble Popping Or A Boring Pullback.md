# AI Bubble Popping Or A Boring Pullback?
**Podcast:** Macro trading floor

---

## AI Executive Summary
1. Core Thesis:
The podcast discusses a shift in the macro economic landscape where traditional yield dynamics have inverted—bonds are now rallying rather than selling off—and questions whether this signals true market equilibrium or underlying fragility within the AI-driven capex cycle. While US core nominal growth remains historically strong (~6.5%) and tech sectors maintain high borrowing returns (20-30% earnings), the hosts argue that aggregate demand is still inconsistent with 2% core inflation targets, implying rates must rise to restore Federal Reserve credibility. The key debate centers on whether the current financial conditions are sufficient or if the market will enforce tighter yields to cool the economy, despite strong AI-related growth fundamentals.

2. Key Arguments & Data:
*   **Bond Correlation Inversion:** Historically, bond selloffs coincided with equity gains (energy effect). Currently, bond rallies provide portfolio hedging and positive carry as they move opposite to equities due to economic concerns.
*   **AI Growth Model:** The economy operates on a "one-legged stool" model driven by AI capex; wealth from the stock market fuels consumer spending, but the long-term viability relies on unproven productivity booms that could commoditize products (open source/free alternatives from China).
*   **US Economic Fundamentals:**
    *   Nominal GDP growth hit 6.5% last quarter (strongest since 1996), driven largely by core consumer spending and tech equipment.
    *   Earnings for major hyperscalers are growing at 20-30% year-on-year.
    *   Hyperscalers borrow at ~7%, appearing solvent, but smaller or riskier players (e.g., CoreWeave) show CDS blowouts.
*   **Inflation & The Fed:**
    *   Core PC is calculated at approximately 3.1% annualized, implying a path toward 2% target requires tighter financial conditions.
    *   The Federal Reserve's Financial Condition Index remains net accommodative despite high inflation data, leading to expectations of minimal rate hikes (<5bps) in October.
    *   If the Fed fails to tighten as required by the data, the market will likely enforce this via higher yields and a stronger dollar.
*   **Credit Risks:** The AI credit boom is comparable to the 2005-2007 mortgage boom; roughly $400B-$500B of new debt (inflation-adjusted) is projected next year, which could lead to significant over-leveraged consequences.
*   **Currency Markets:**
    *   USD/CAD has a "good news, bad price" setup; CAD weakens due to poor seasonality adjustments despite strong economic data.
    *   Euro faces volatility around the French budget vote and expected Moody's downgrade (rated at 160bps two Fridays ago).
    *   Market positioning in the USD shows danger zone of too many longs, suggesting potential adjustment soon.

3. Actionable Takeaways:
*   **Portfolio Construction:** Integrate bonds into your portfolio to capture positive carry and hedge against equity volatility, as the inverse correlation between bonds and equities is currently beneficial.
*   **Fed Policy Monitoring:** Focus on the October Fed meeting and Core PC data; anticipate that true equilibrium requires higher yields to cool aggregate demand, even if the market currently expects a dovish outcome.
*   **AI Cycle Risk Management:** Acknowledge binary risks in AI credit cycles; while hyperscalers are profitable, do not ignore potential systemic fragility from interconnected financial structures or bankruptcies (e.g., CoreWeave scenario).
*   **Currency Opportunities:** Consider short positions in USD and long positions in EUR as the "good news, bad price" dynamic favors a USD correction, especially pending French budget vote and Moody's actions.
*   **Avoiding Market Noise:** Do not rely on binary crisis narratives (e.g., 2008 vs. AI catastrophe); instead, treat the situation as a tall tower of debt that is wobbling but requires structural tightening to stabilize.
