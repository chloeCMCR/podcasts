# The Certainty Premium: What’s Driving the Tech Sector Divergence? | Jeff Keller
**Podcast:** Monetary matters

---

## AI Executive Summary
### 1. Core Thesis
The market currently operates under high long-term uncertainty, driving a significant premium for assets offering short-term certainty. While the AI adoption narrative is confirmed as an open-ended growth trajectory (similar to the newspaper industry) rather than a cyclical blow-off top, retail involvement and sector volatility signal increased risk of correction. Jeff Keller emphasizes that investors should prioritize long-term visibility (3+ year horizon) over short-term trading momentum, focusing on fundamental metrics like ARR and CapEx rather than speculative multiples or hype.

### 2. Key Arguments & Data
*   **Market Environment:**
    *   High long-term uncertainty creates a discount premium for short-term certainty; anything predictable is trading at a high price.
    *   Heavy retail involvement in a trade (e.g., crypto, gold, software in Q3/2021) often precedes the end of that trade cycle.
    *   S&P 11 Sector Performance (Q3 Start): Software and Services up ~20%; Technology Hardware/Equipment flat; Semiconductors down ~7.5%.
    *   Tech sector underperformed Industrials and Utilities in terms of recent performance relative to the AI trade laggards.
*   **Sector Analysis:**
    *   **Software:** Diverged between Application Software (questioned due to low double-digit growth vs. high revenue multiples) and Cyber/Infra (performing well due to usage-based certainty).
    *   **Cybersecurity:** Trading at high premiums (25-30x revenue); driven by fear of AI disruption threats; currently outperforming but remains a "paradigm shift" subject to TDA.
    *   **Infrastructure:** Database and CDN players benefitting from software proliferation; usage-based revenue models favored in current market.
    *   **Hyperscalers (Mag7):** Google reported $18.8B negative adjusted free cash flow (2026). Market views this as "optionality" purchase rather than ROI failure (capex to secure future dominance against 10 competitors vs. previous 3).
    *   **Semiconductors:** Low multiple names outperforming high multiple ones (e.g., Nvidia) due to same cyclical drivers but different growth rates; priced in rosier futures than hardware leaders.
*   **Valuation & Risk Factors:**
    *   One-to-two quarter extrapolation is dangerous; 3-year visibility allows for better downside protection with decent earnings multiples.
    *   Capital markets: Concerns about bond yields crowding out treasury markets; Silicon Valley leaders have raised $500B+ (e.g., Labs, SpaceX) and can self-fund buildouts despite credit tightening.
    *   Fed Policy: Market expects hiking by end of 2026 but not with the severity of 2022 rates; rate sensitivity remains a concern for real businesses funding via debt.
    *   **AI Feedback Loop:** Market reaction to partnership deals dampened compared to 2021 due to skepticism on sustainability of long-term profits (e.g., crypto miners converting to data centers).
*   **Investment Strategy & Portfolio:**
    *   **Long-Short Model:** Long-biased (>50% net exposure) with duration; benefits from capturing beta without penalty fees of neutral strategies.
    *   **Leverage Preference:** Low leverage to avoid maxed-out positions during volatility (e.g., Korean margin calls in 2024).
    *   **Tech vs. Speculative:** Dismissing the "2021 blow-off top" comparison as disingenuous; AI businesses have different fundamentals than SPACs/Arthos names.
    *   **AI Tools Impact:** AI-generated content creates investor echo chambers and false fundamental confidence, increasing market inefficiencies faster than speed improvements in quantitative models.

### 3. Actionable Takeaways
*   **Sector Picking:** Overweight infrastructure and cyber due to usage-based certainty and short-term revenue visibility; avoid speculative application software growth with high non-recurring revenue risks unless validated by disruption narratives (like the newspaper analogy).
*   **Investment Horizon:** Focus on a 3-year horizon for technology valuations; resist 1-2 quarter trading based on technical catalysts as volatility often obscures fundamentals.
*   **Risk Management:** Monitor sector-specific deleveraging signals (e.g., Korea margin calls) to identify contrarian buying opportunities during temporary panics or "information vacuums."
*   **Portfolio Construction:** Maintain optionality (cash or lower gross exposure) rather than full leverage in volatile environments; avoid maxed positions during standard deviation events.
*   **Metric Focus:** Prioritize ARR, adoption curves, CapEx levels, and forward compute prices over immediate stock price momentum or press release hype when evaluating AI leaders.
*   **Hedge Against Uncertainty:** Acknowledge that market efficiency is decreasing due to retail involvement and AI-generated echo chambers; rely on bottom-up fundamental work rather than thematic momentum bets.
