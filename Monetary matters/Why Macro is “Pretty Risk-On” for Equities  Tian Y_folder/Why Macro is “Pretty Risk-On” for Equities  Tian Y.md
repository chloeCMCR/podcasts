# Why Macro is “Pretty Risk-On” for Equities | Tian Yang of Variant Perception
**Podcast:** Monetary matters

## AI Executive Summary
This transcript features a deep-dive macro discussion between an interviewer and **Tien**, a strategist from **Variant Perception**. The conversation covers China’s structural economic issues, the evolving landscape of global AI, geopolitical shifts in emerging markets, and specific investment strategies.

Here is a thematic summary of the key insights:

### 1. The China Dilemma: Supply-Side Focus vs. Domestic Consumption
Tien argues that China’s economic weakness is not new but structural. 
* **Crowding Out Households:** For 15 years, China has prioritized the "sovereign" agenda (industry and tech), which has inherently crowded out the household sector. This, combined with the real estate crash (destroying household wealth), makes a consumer-led rebound unlikely without a massive, improbable revaluation of the Renminbi.
* **The Export Trap:** Because China focuses on the supply side to maintain employment, it inevitably creates excess capacity. To avoid domestic stagnation, China must export this excess, leading to global trade tensions and "price wars" (e.g., in EVs and manufacturing).
* **Corporate Profits:** While the government suggests an "anti-involution" (reducing cutthroat competition) approach for giants like Alibaba and Tencent, Tien believes it is difficult to implement as long as there isn't enough domestic demand to match the supply.

### 2. The Future of AI: Tiers and Profit Pools
The discussion moves from Chinese macro to the global AI landscape, focusing on how intelligence will be commoditized.
* **Three-Tiered Intelligence Model:** Tien predicts a market split into three distinct layers:
    1.  **Mass Market (150 IQ):** Open-source, low marginal cost, highly commoditized. This is where China excels and where profit pools are destroyed.
    2.  **Business/Enterprise (150–200 IQ):** Focused on workflow automation and efficiency.
    3.  **Elite/Sovereign (200+ IQ):** High-end, high-trust models for governments and major corporations. This is where the real profit and "moats" (data trust/cloud ecosystems) will reside.
* **The China Factor:** China’s strategy is to adopt open-source models to prevent being "locked in" to US ecosystems. While this accelerates global price compression, it serves China's national interest by gaining market share through low cost and rapid commercialization.

### 3. Global Macro: Geopolitical Winners and Losers
Tien provides a specific outlook on several key regions based on their geopolitical positioning:
* **India (The Vulnerable):** Unlike the "carrot" of Latin America, India is caught between the "sticks" of the US and China. As India tries to build its manufacturing base, it may face resistance from both superpowers who view its rise as a potential threat.
/
* **Latin America (The Opportunity):** Viewed as a "carrot" that both the US and China want to attract, making it a more attractive allocation.
* **Europe (The Struggler):** Faces significant headwinds from energy crises, heavy regulation, and increasing competition from Chinese exports (e.g., EVs).
* **Japan (The Unstable):** The macro situation is "untenable." Despite rising inflation on the ground (wages/real estate), the Bank of Japan’s (BOJ) attempt to manage the currency without meaningful rate hikes or fiscal coordination is seen as a struggle for credibility.

### 4. Monetary Policy and Commodities
* **US Federal Reserve:** The focus shouldn't just be on incremental rate hikes, but on **restoring market functioning.** The goal should be removing excess reserves and allowing money markets to provide proper price signals again.
* **Gold & Metals:** Gold has likely found its floor; while it serves as a good hedge against stagflation, a massive rally requires central banks to begin cutting rates. However, other metals remain a "bullish" play due to ongoing geopolitical volatility.

### 5. Investment Strategy: The VPX ETF
Tien explains the mechanics behind their **VPX (Varying Perception US Equity ETF)**:
* **Methodology:** A systematic, long-only strategy that avoids the "concentration risk" of the S&P 500 (where a few tech names drive everything). It uses a combination of **Capital Cycle, Quality, and Crowding** models to rotate sectors.
* **Performance Drivers:** The fund's recent outperformance is attributed to **factor timing**—specifically moving from heavy Tech/Growth exposure in April/May into Energy and Value as market volatility increased and the "AI factor" became more unstable.
