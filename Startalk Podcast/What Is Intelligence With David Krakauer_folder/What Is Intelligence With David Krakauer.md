# What Is Intelligence? With David Krakauer
**Podcast:** Startalk Podcast

---

## AI Executive Summary
### 1. Core Thesis
The episode argues that intelligence is a universal biological trait ("making hard problems easy") rather than a binary human-only attribute, distinguishing human evolutionary intelligence from artificial intelligence (AI) through concepts of efficiency and energy cost. While AI excels at solving problems via vast data processing (unconscious-like simulation), it lacks the biological constraints—energy scarcity and rapid decision-making—that drive true problem-solving evolution. The central thesis is that true superintelligence will likely emerge through universal Darwinism rather than computational overload; therefore, humans must prioritize retaining cognitive competence and curiosity over outsourcing calculation to avoid losing intelligibility and survival capability in an increasingly automated world.

### 2. Key Arguments & Data
*   **Definition of Intelligence:**
    *   **Biological Continuum:** Intelligence is not binary (alive/dead); all life forms possess a form of intelligence defined by the ability to sense, act adaptively, and solve problems in their environment.
    *   **Universal Constraint:** Evolutionary processes are constrained by energy limits; one bit of information can be added to a genome per generation because selection mechanisms cannot differentiate multiple simultaneous changes without confusion (bricks at a time).
    *   **Brain vs. Genome:** Brains evolved specifically to outpace the slow speed of genetic evolution to solve environmental problems in real-time when resources are scarce.

*   **Human Language vs. Animal Signaling:**
    *   **Infinite Use of Finite Means:** Humans can generate arbitrary complexity through grammar (symbols), whereas non-human signaling is often direct index-to-response correlations or icons (e.g., alarm calls, chemical markers).
    *   **Decoupling:** Human language decouples thought from immediate sensory context, allowing for abstract deliberation but also creating "great stupidity" when misused.

*   **AI vs. Biological Intelligence:**
    *   **Inefficiency:** AI/LLMs solve problems by brute force (energy/time cost) rather than biological efficiency; they are inefficient because they operate outside human constraints like hunger and death.
    *   **Heuristics vs. Algorithms:** LLMs use statistical heuristics that fail at scale compared to human-trained algorithms (e.g., long division gets wrong at large scales).
    *   **Lack of Unconscious:** AI has no unconscious problem-solving process; it relies solely on linguistic training data with no biological grounding for intuition or "sleep on it" insights.

*   **Energy and Entropy:**
    *   **Information as Energy:** Processing information costs energy; current LLMs create massive entropy equivalent to the industrial revolution's combustion of fossil fuels (coal/knowledge deposits).
    *   **Cultural Entropy:** The "steam engine" analogy applies to AI: it liberates stored knowledge but creates cultural pollution (misinformation, confabulation) unlike physical coal which does not degrade.
    *   **Complementary vs. Competitive Tools:** Tools are categorized as complementary (enhancing human reason, like the abacus/slide rule where the tool integrates into skill) or competitive (replacing brain function, like GPS/LLMs where dependence grows).

*   **Consciousness & Intelligibility:**
    *   **Intelligence vs. Consciousness:** Intelligence = problem-solving capability; Consciousness = being and feeling (survival of the self).
    *   **Intelligibility > Intelligence:** The goal is not just utility or prediction, but making the universe intelligible. False precision distracts from truth.

### 3. Actionable Takeaways
*   **Assess Capability vs. Competence:** Do not equate AI output with competence; evaluate whether you retain the underlying understanding of how a tool works (e.g., do I still understand significant figures?) to avoid catastrophic failure during a power cut or technological dependency.
*   **Prioritize Questioning over Answering:** As technical capabilities are outsourced to machines, human value lies in asking profound questions ("What are we going to be smart enough for?" "Why does it matter?") rather than calculating answers.
*   **Retain Intuition Skills:** Cultivate skills like approximation, significant figures, and heuristic judgment that cannot be automated. These represent deep "intelligibility" that AI lacks because it is not trained on the curriculum of life's scarcity constraints.
*   **View AI as Complementary Artifacts:** Use AI tools (like protein folding simulations) only to expand human capability where biology fails (high-dimensional problems), but do not rely on them for cognitive tasks requiring intuition and long-term memory.
*   **Guard Against Cultural Entropy:** Be critical of the statistical "crap" generated by LLMs; distinguish between probabilistic association and verified context, recognizing that AI can confabulate solutions to hard problems without understanding them.
